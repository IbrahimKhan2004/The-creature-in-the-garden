A Short Story

The Creature
in the Garden

Some monsters only exist in the eye of the beholder.

Begin  ↓

Part One  ·  The Chase

David had spent the previous night arguing with everyone.

His wife, Sarah. His son. His daughter.

By midnight, the house was silent, but the bitterness remained.

The next day was his birthday.

The family still organized a small celebration in their garden. There was cake, food, and forced smiles. David sat beside Sarah, pretending everything was normal.

That was when he saw it.

Near the hedge stood a creature unlike anything he had ever seen. It resembled a cat, but it was far too large. Its fur was pitch black, swallowing the sunlight around it. Its yellow eyes stared directly at him.

David immediately looked away.

Maybe he was imagining things.

When he glanced back, the creature was still there.

Nobody else seemed to notice it.

The family continued celebrating as if nothing was wrong.

Minutes later, he looked again.

The creature had moved closer.

Then it took another step.

And another.

Panic gripped him.

David jumped from his chair and ran.

The creature followed.

Through the garden.

Through the house.

Past confused relatives calling his name.

David could hear footsteps behind him. He climbed through a window and jumped onto a neighboring property. Hiding behind a wall, he struggled to breathe.

A man eventually found him And Asked Him.

"What are you doing here?"

David ignored him and Run and he climbed back down.

As he re entered his house next door, his father stared at him.

"What on earth is wrong with you?"

Then David saw movement.

The back door slowly opened.

The creature stepped inside.

Its eyes locked onto him.

David ran again. Out onto the street. A motorcyclist nearly collided with him. In a desperate panic, David kicked the rider away and sped off on the bike.

The creature remained behind him.

Always there.

Always following.

✦ ✦ ✦

The chase continued until David reached an old bridge. He abandoned the motorcycle and stumbled toward the center of it.

The creature emerged from the darkness.

For the first time, it wasn't running.

It approached slowly. Carefully. Its head lowered slightly. Its eyes no longer seemed threatening.

They seemed…

sad.

David's hand found a knife lying nearby.

The creature took another step forward.

Then another.

As if it wanted to come closer. As if it wanted to say something.

But fear had already won.

David screamed and charged. The knife plunged into the creature's chest. Once. Twice. Again and again.

The creature never fought back.

It simply stared at him. Almost disappointed. Almost heartbroken.

As its body collapsed onto the bridge, David staggered backward.

Then something strange happened.

The black fur began to disappear.

The enormous body started changing shape.

Shrinking.

Twisting.

Becoming human.

David froze. His hands trembled. The knife slipped from his fingers.

Lying in a pool of blood before him was Sarah.

His wife.

The same woman he had argued with the night before. The same woman who had been sitting beside him at the birthday party.

Sarah looked up at him one final time. There was no anger in her eyes. Only sadness. Then she stopped moving.

David dropped to his knees.

"No…"

The realization hit him all at once. The creature had never tried to attack him. It had followed him everywhere but never harmed him. It had only been trying to reach him. Trying to make him stop running. Trying to make him understand.

Just like Sarah always had.

And just like every argument they ever had, David had assumed the worst before knowing the truth.

Darkness swallowed him.
✦ ✦ ✦
Part Two  ·  The Hospital

David opened his eyes in a hospital bed. Machines beeped around him. His father sat nearby. His children were there too.

But Sarah wasn't.

A doctor entered the room.

"You're lucky to be alive."

David looked around.

"Where's Sarah?"

The room fell silent. Nobody answered.

The doctor's expression became uneasy.

"You suffered a severe psychological breakdown during your birthday celebration."

David felt his heart racing.

"You ran through several properties, stole a motorcycle, and crashed near the bridge."

David shook his head.

"No. Sarah was there. The creature… it became Sarah."

The doctor exchanged glances with David's father. Then he spoke quietly.

"There was never any creature."

David stared at him.

"Witnesses say your wife chased after you the entire time, trying to stop you. Near the bridge, during the struggle, you grabbed a knife."

David's blood turned cold.

"No…"

His father began crying.

The doctor lowered his head.

"Sarah died from the stab wound."

The room became silent. David sat frozen.

The monster.

The chase.

The fear.

None of it had been real.

The creature he spent the entire day running from was only the distorted image of the person who loved him most.

And in trying to destroy what he feared…

he had killed his own wife.

Some monsters are only the shapes love takes
when we are too afraid to look clearly.
